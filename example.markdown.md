# Adult vs Baby Diaper Sales in Japan: Trends and Context

## Market Overview – Adult vs Baby Diaper Sales

Japan's rapidly aging society has led to adult diapers now routinely outselling baby diapers. In fact, adult diaper sales have outpaced infant diaper sales in Japan for well over a decade. Recent industry data shows this gap is widening: Japan's adult diaper market is projected to grow to about ¥98.9 billion (by 2027), whereas the baby diaper market is expected to shrink to around ¥84.6 billion. Many manufacturers already report higher earnings from adult incontinence products – for example, Daio Paper says its adult diaper revenue is roughly double that of baby diapers in its product line. These figures underscore a major shift in the domestic diaper industry toward products for seniors.

## Historical Milestones in Diaper Sales Shift

-   **2011**: Japan's largest diaper maker, Unicharm, revealed that sales of adult diapers had overtaken those of baby diapers in its portfolio. This "tipping point" occurred much earlier than expected – the Nikkei had projected adult diapers would surpass baby diapers by 2020, but Unicharm's data showed it happened in 2011.

-   **Mid-2010s**: As the trend accelerated, manufacturers expanded adult diaper production. By 2016, with over a quarter of Japan's population above age 65, more diapers were being produced for adults than for babies nationwide. This was a clear indicator that the market had decisively shifted towards senior consumers.

-   **2020s**: Adult diapers firmly outsold baby diapers every year, reflecting entrenched demographic changes. Oji Holdings – a major paper products company – made headlines in 2024 by announcing it would stop making baby diapers in Japan to focus on adult diapers, after its infant diaper output fell from a peak of ~700 million units in 2001 to only 400 million in recent years. This move highlights how declining births have forced companies to pivot strategies. Likewise, competitors like Daio Paper have stated that the future growth is in adult diapers and are devoting more resources to that sector. These milestones mark the industry's adaptation to Japan's aging population.

## Demographic and Societal Drivers

Several demographic factors explain why adult diapers now outsell baby diapers in Japan:

-   **Aging Population**: Japan has the oldest population in the world. Nearly 30% of Japanese residents are 65 or older, and in 2022 the share of people over 80 years old exceeded 10% for the first time. This large senior population creates a robust and growing demand for adult incontinence products.

-   **Declining Birth Rate**: Births in Japan have plummeted to record lows. Only 758,631 babies were born in 2023, a historic low (down 5% from the previous year). For comparison, annual births were over 2 million in the 1970s. Today, children under 15 make up less than 12% of the population. Fewer babies mean shrinking demand for infant diapers, contributing to the baby diaper market's contraction.

-   **Population Decline**: Deaths now far outnumber births each year, and Japan's total population is in decline. Projections show a drop from about 125 million people today to roughly 87–88 million by 2065–2070 (a ~30% decline). By that time, an estimated 40% of the population could be senior citizens. This forecast indicates the imbalance will only grow, further boosting adult diaper demand while the youth market shrinks.

-   **Societal Shifts**: Longer life expectancies and improved elder care have expanded the market of active seniors who use convenience products. At the same time, factors like high child-rearing costs, later marriages, and more women in the workforce have contributed to fewer births. These societal trends reinforce the demographic tilt toward an older society, directly impacting diaper sales.

## Industry Response and Notable Insights

Major diaper manufacturers in Japan have adjusted to these demographic realities:

-   **Unicharm** (the market leader) began pivoting over a decade ago, as evidenced by its 2011 report of adult diapers outselling baby versions. This early recognition of the trend allowed Unicharm to capitalize on the growing senior market sooner than rivals.

-   **Daio Paper** has been investing heavily in adult diaper production. Company representatives note that they expect continued growth in adult diaper demand and are reallocating resources accordingly. Daio's marketing emphasizes improved comfort and discretion for adult users, acknowledging that older consumers are more discerning than infants and can be loyal customers if their needs are met.

-   **Oji Holdings** took a dramatic step in 2024 by ceasing domestic baby diaper production altogether, shifting focus entirely to adult diapers. The firm cited the persistent decline in Japan's birth rate as the reason, and it plans to serve the infant market only overseas (in countries with higher birth rates). Oji's exit from the baby segment in Japan is a telling milestone of how far the balance has tipped.

These industry moves underscore a consensus: the growth opportunities in Japan lie with products for an aging population, not infants. The adult diaper market has been expanding (estimated at over $2 billion in annual sales now), providing a vital revenue stream as the baby diaper business contracts. Manufacturers like Unicharm, Daio, and Oji have adapted their strategies to serve the needs of Japan's seniors, from developing high-absorbency, comfortable "pants-type" adult diapers to marketing them in a way that reduces stigma for elderly customers.

## Conclusion

Adult diapers overtaking baby diapers in sales is both a symbol and a direct consequence of Japan's demographic shift. This trend first emerged in the early 2010s and has only strengthened since. An aging population (with unprecedented numbers of elderly) combined with chronically low birth rates has flipped a consumer goods market upside-down. What used to be a niche product for nursing homes is now a mainstream business that outpaces baby products. Japan's case is closely watched by other countries facing aging societies, as it highlights how population changes can fundamentally alter market demand. The notable milestones – from Unicharm's 2011 crossover to a major firm like Oji exiting the baby diaper market – illustrate the speed and scale of this change. Backed by hard statistics and company reports, the sales of adult diapers now exceed those of infant diapers in Japan, a trend likely to continue in the foreseeable future given current demographic trajectories.
